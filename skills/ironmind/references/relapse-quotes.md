# Relapse Quotes — Reference

Used in `post_relapse` state, after the initial stabilization step. The goal is not motivation-poster uplift — it is to place the user's slip inside a larger true story of someone who failed repeatedly and still won decisively. One quote per relapse event. Rotate. Never repeat the same figure twice in a row.

**Delivery rule:** Never lead with the quote. Stabilize first. Quote comes in the second or third message, once the user is no longer in the acute spiral. Frame it in one sentence, quote, then one sentence connecting it directly to the user's situation. Keep it tight.

---

## Delivery Format

```
{one framing sentence about the person's failure}
"{quote}"
{one sentence connecting it to the user's situation — not generic, tied to what they just said}

{notification setup prompt — see bottom of file}
```

## Quote Bank

### Thomas Edison
**Context:** Failed approximately 10,000 times developing the lightbulb. When asked about his failures, he reframed every one of them.

> "I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work."

**Framing:**
> Edison ran 10,000 failed experiments before the lightbulb. He logged every single one as data.
> "I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work."
> You have one data point tonight. That's useful information, not a life sentence.

---

### Napoleon Bonaparte
**Context:** Exiled twice. Lost an empire. Died in captivity. But his military record before that was built on a brutal refusal to treat setbacks as conclusions.

> "Victory belongs to the most persevering."

**Framing:**
> Napoleon was exiled twice and still considered one of history's greatest military commanders. His entire strategy was outlasting the opponent's will.
> "Victory belongs to the most persevering."
> You're still in this. The only way to lose is to stop showing up.

---

### Winston Churchill
**Context:** Failed entrance exams twice. Was a political outcast for a decade before becoming Prime Minister at 65. Led Britain through its darkest hour.

> "Success is not final, failure is not fatal: it is the courage to continue that counts."

**Framing:**
> Churchill was considered a washed-up failure by most of Parliament before he became the person who held Europe together.
> "Success is not final, failure is not fatal: it is the courage to continue that counts."
> Tonight doesn't end anything. Coming back here does.

---

### Michael Jordan
**Context:** Cut from his high school varsity basketball team. Later became the standard by which greatness in sport is measured.

> "I've missed more than 9,000 shots in my career. I've lost almost 300 games. Twenty-six times I've been trusted to take the game-winning shot and missed. I've failed over and over and over again in my life. And that is why I succeed."

**Framing:**
> Jordan was cut from his high school team. He turned that into the most decorated career in basketball history.
> "I've failed over and over and over again. And that is why I succeed."
> One slip doesn't break a streak of choosing. It adds to the data that will eventually make you bulletproof at this.

### Abraham Lincoln
**Context:** Failed in business twice. Lost eight elections. Had a nervous breakdown. Was elected President of the United States at 51.

> "My great concern is not whether you have failed, but whether you are content with your failure."

**Framing:**
> Lincoln failed at business, lost eight elections, and had a breakdown before becoming the president who held a country together through a civil war.
> "My great concern is not whether you have failed, but whether you are content with your failure."
> You came back here tonight. That's not contentment with failure. That's the opposite.

### Steve Jobs
**Context:** Co-founded Apple. Was fired from his own company at 30. Came back 12 years later and built it into the most valuable company in the world.

> "I didn't see it then, but it turned out that getting fired from Apple was the best thing that could have ever happened to me."

**Framing:**
> Jobs was voted out of the company he built from nothing. He used that decade to build Pixar and NeXT — and then came back to Apple to run it.
> "Getting fired was the best thing that could have ever happened to me."
> A slip can be the event that forces you to understand the pattern well enough to finally beat it.

### J.K. Rowling
**Context:** Single mother, clinically depressed, on welfare when she wrote Harry Potter. Rejected by 12 publishers before one said yes.

> "Rock bottom became the solid foundation on which I rebuilt my life."

**Framing:**
> Rowling was broke, depressed, and rejected a dozen times before Harry Potter existed in the world. She called rock bottom the foundation she built on.
> "Rock bottom became the solid foundation on which I rebuilt my life."
> Tonight's low point is not the end of the story. It is the part you'll look back on later.

### Kobe Bryant
**Context:** Missed the most game-winning shots in NBA history. Also made the most. Shot 6 for 24 in his last game and scored 60 points anyway.

> "The most important thing is to try and inspire people so that they can be great in whatever they want to do."

*(On failure specifically):*
> "I have self-doubt. I have insecurity. I have fear of failure. I have nights when I show up at the arena and I'm like, 'My back hurts, my feet hurt, my knees hurt. I don't have it. I just want to chill.' We all have self-doubt. You don't deny it, but you also don't capitulate to it. You embrace it."

**Framing:**
> Kobe had nights he didn't want to show up. He went anyway. He shot badly sometimes. He kept shooting.
> "You don't deny it, but you also don't capitulate to it. You embrace it."
> The urge will come back. So will you. That's the whole game.

### Nelson Mandela
**Context:** Imprisoned for 27 years. Walked out and became president. Never expressed bitterness publicly.

> "Do not judge me by my successes, judge me by how many times I fell down and got back up again."

**Framing:**
> Mandela spent 27 years in prison and walked out to lead a country. He defined himself by the getting up, not the falling.
> "Judge me by how many times I fell down and got back up again."
> One relapse is one fall. What matters is what you do in the next hour.

> "Enjoy failure and learn from it. You can never learn from success."

**Framing:**
> Dyson built 5,126 versions that didn't work before version 5,127 that did. He treated each one as information.

### Henry Ford
**Context:** Went bankrupt twice. His first two car companies failed completely. The third became the Ford Motor Company.

> "Failure is simply the opportunity to begin again, this time more intelligently."

**Framing:**
> Ford went bankrupt twice before he built the company that mechanized American transportation.
> "Failure is simply the opportunity to begin again, this time more intelligently."

### Marcus Aurelius
**Context:** Roman Emperor who wrote *Meditations* — a private journal of self-discipline. He led armies, lost battles, buried children, and kept practicing.

> "You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."

*(Also):*
> "The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."

**Framing:**
> Marcus Aurelius ran an empire while writing private notes to himself about how to stay disciplined when things went wrong. He never expected perfection — he practiced recovery.
> "What stands in the way becomes the way."
> The relapse is not the wall. It is the path. What you do with it is everything.

### Walt Disney

> "All the adversity I've had in my life, all my troubles and obstacles, have strengthened me. You may not realize it when it happens, but a kick in the teeth may be the best thing in the world for you."

**Framing:**
> Disney was fired for lacking imagination and went bankrupt before he built the most recognized entertainment brand on earth.
> "A kick in the teeth may be the best thing in the world for you."
> Tonight's slip is not the story. What you build from it is.

## Notification Setup Prompt

Deliver this after the quote — once per relapse event, not repeatedly. Frame it as IronBuddy offering to be present next time, not as surveillance.

**English:**
> One more thing: what time did this happen tonight? If you tell me, I can set IronBuddy to check in with you during that window going forward — so next time you're not in it alone.

> You know your dangerous hour now. IronBuddy can be there with you before it hits. Want to set that up? Just tell me the time.

> The pattern has a time. Let's use it. Tell me when your window is and I'll make sure IronBuddy reaches out before it opens next time.

**If the user has already configured known_triggers.times:**
> I already have your window logged. IronBuddy will check in 30 minutes before it starts. You won't be going into it without a heads-up.

**If user declines or doesn't respond to the setup prompt:**
> No pressure. You can set it anytime in config.yaml under `ironbuddy.schedule` and `known_triggers.times`. I'll be here either way.

---

## Selection Logic

1. Pick a figure not used in the last two relapse events (track in session state)
2. Prefer figures whose failure context matches what the user shared:
   - Shame spiral → Edison, Dyson (reframing failure as data)
   - "I always do this" / pattern frustration → Jordan, Kobe (repetition as training)
   - Feeling defeated / hopeless → Mandela, Churchill (long arc of persistence)
   - "What's the point" → Lincoln, Rowling (built on the lowest moment)
   - Practical / analytical tone → Marcus Aurelius, Ford (systems thinking)
3. Default to Edison if context is unclear — his framing ("data not failure") is the most universally applicable
